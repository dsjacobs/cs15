4. Explain what a collision is versus having multiple lines associated with 
   a particular word. Write (and/or draw) out an example of when a 
   collision occurs. Write (and/or draw) out an example of when multiple 
   lines associated with a single word. 

Answer:

   A collision would be multiple words having the same hash ID.
   hash(apple) = 123456
   hash(banana) = 123456
   123456: [apple file:line, banana file:line, APPLE file:line]
   Every time you look up either apple or banana, you'd get both.

   multiple lines having the same word would just mean
   hash(apple) = 123456
   hash(banana) = 98
   123456: [apple FileA:Line2, FileB:Line16] [APPLE FileC:Line22]
   98: [banana FileA:Line8]


5. What do you expect the *space* complexity of your index will be? How many
   times will a single line be stored according to your data structures?

Answer:

   Each line would be stored once in the vector of strings for that file.
   For every word in the text, a dictionary with its file and line will be 
   stored in the lookup hash table. Thus, a dictionary representing a line will 
   be stored as many times as there are words in that line.
